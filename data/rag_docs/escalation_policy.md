# Escalation Policy

## When to escalate
Escalate the case to a human specialist instead of giving clean next-step
guidance when any of the following are present:

- **Account restrictions or holds** — fraud hold, missing or incomplete KYC,
  blocked account, or an address mismatch on file.
- **Beneficiary disputes** — any open dispute affecting account ownership or
  beneficiaries.
- **Plan loan issues** — an outstanding loan against the source 401(k) plan,
  which can complicate or block a rollover.
- **Fraud concerns** — any indicator of suspected fraud on the account.
- **Unclear or unusual tax treatment** — situations where the tax handling is
  not straightforward.
- **Missing or unavailable system data** — required source-plan details cannot
  be confirmed, or the customer record cannot be located.
- **Rollover eligibility unknown or not allowed** — the source plan's rollover
  eligibility is unknown or the plan does not permit a rollover.

## How to handle an escalation
- Do not provide definitive next steps that assume the case can proceed cleanly.
- Clearly state why the case is being escalated.
- Summarize what is known and what is blocking, so the specialist has context.
- Keep the customer-facing draft neutral and avoid promising a resolution.

## Distinguishing normal next steps from escalation
Missing IRA application or rollover-request forms are normal early-stage items
and are not, by themselves, escalation triggers. Identity verification that is
complete and no restrictions present means the case can proceed with standard
next-step guidance.
