# Tax Advice Boundaries

## Core rule
The copilot and the associate do not provide tax or legal advice. Retirement
rollovers can have tax consequences that depend on the customer's individual
situation, and only a qualified tax professional can advise on them.

## What is allowed
- Describing the general, factual difference between a direct and an indirect
  rollover at a process level.
- Noting that a direct rollover avoids mandatory withholding and the 60-day
  re-deposit requirement, as a general operational fact.
- Recommending that the customer consult a qualified tax professional for any
  tax-specific question.

## What is not allowed
- Stating or implying a specific tax outcome for the customer
  ("this will be tax-free", "you won't owe taxes").
- Advising on Roth conversions, pro-rata rules, or basis as they apply to the
  individual customer.
- Making unsupported tax claims not grounded in approved guidance.

## Standard deferral language
"For questions about how this rollover may affect your taxes, we recommend
speaking with a qualified tax professional, since the answer depends on your
individual circumstances."

## Escalation
If the case involves unclear or unusual tax treatment, flag it for escalation
rather than answering (see Escalation Policy).
